Everything, both ways

Not just a remote screen.
Your two devices, as one.

Drive the computer from the phone, see the phone on the computer, and let copies, files and messages cross on their own.

Your screen, at low latency

Hardware H.264 or HEVC, sent over QUIC and decoded by the phone's own chip. When the link slows, the picture stays recent: stale frames are dropped and the rate follows what gets through.

Yours alone

No account. No cloud.
Just your two devices.

Pocketdesk runs no server of its own. The phone and the computer hold each other to their keys, and you decide what each phone may do.

Pinned keys, TLS 1.3

Scan the QR code once. From then on, each side checks the other's certificate before a single byte is sent.

Permissions per phone

Each phone gets its own switches for input, files, clipboard and more. You can be asked before every session.

Private copies stay put

A copy a password manager marks private never leaves the computer. Neither do copies from apps you list.

Everything on the record

The protocol is documented in full: what is sent, and to whom. The code is yours to read under MIT or Apache-2.0.

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. Pocketdesk is open source under MIT or Apache-2.0, with no paid tier, no ads and no account.

Does my screen go through your servers?

No. There is no Pocketdesk server. At home the phone talks to the computer directly. Away from home, iroh finds a direct path, and when it can't, a relay carries the encrypted traffic. A relay sees which addresses talk and how much, never the content. You can run your own relay.

Do I need to set up my router?

No. After one session at home (or through Tailscale), the phone reaches the computer from anywhere without a port forward. Only waking a sleeping computer from outside needs one, or another always-on computer at home.

Which phones and computers does it run on?

The app runs on Android 8 and newer. The computer side runs on Windows 10 and 11 and on Linux: Wayland (Hyprland, Sway, GNOME, KDE) and X11. iPhone and macOS aren't supported.

Does it need a graphics card?

It uses your GPU's video encoder when there is one (NVENC, AMF, Quick Sync or VAAPI) and falls back to software encoding when there isn't.

Can someone else take over my computer?

Only a phone you paired, and only with the permissions you gave it. Pairing checks each side's certificate, and you can have the computer ask before every session.
